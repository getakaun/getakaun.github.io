Outstanding, partially paid and paid

Some records are paid when they happen. Other records stay outstanding until a payment comes later. This page tells how Akaun knows which records are paid.

Paid now, or outstanding until later​

A record that moves money straight from a cash or bank account is paid when you save it. For example, you pay 50.00 from Bank for software. The record shows Paid immediately.

A record that uses Accounts Payable or Accounts Receivable is outstanding. The money did not move yet:

  • Accounts Payable holds the payables. A payable is money that the business owes to a contact. For example, a supplier sends a bill of 300.00, and you pay it next month.
  • Accounts Receivable holds the receivables. A receivable is money that a contact owes to the business. For example, you send an invoice of 500.00 to a customer.

Such a record must name a contact. Akaun then knows who owes the money, or to whom the business owes it. See Contacts and their balances.

The statuses​

Each record has one of these statuses. The page of a transfer shows no status.

StatusWhat it shows
OutstandingNobody paid any of the amount yet.
Partially paidPayments cover some of the amount, but not all of it. The record page shows the amount that is still outstanding.
PaidPayments cover the full amount, or the record did not use Accounts Payable or Accounts Receivable.

The Records screen has a tab for each status: All, Outstanding, Partially paid and Paid.

Akaun calculates the status​

You cannot mark a record as paid. Akaun calculates the status from the payments against the record. Thus the status on each screen is always the same.

A payment is a record of its own. It moves money out of a cash or bank account and puts it against one or more outstanding records. The part of a payment that goes against one record is an allocation. When you record a payment, the Allocation section shows each outstanding record and its allocation.

For example, a supplier bill is 300.00. You pay 100.00. The bill shows Partially paid, with 200.00 outstanding. Later you pay 200.00. The bill then shows Paid.

A payment cannot put more against a record than the amount that is outstanding on it.

One payment for many records​

One payment can settle many records. For example, a supplier sends three bills in one month. You pay all three with one bank transfer.

For company-paid expenses, Auto import or + Add record records the expense and bank payment together, with the supplier as its contact. There is no additional payment step.

For expenses someone personally paid, select Accounts payable as the source account when recording the expense or reviewing an import. Keep the supplier as the contact. Accounts payable expenses are treated as personally paid; no additional payment-source field is needed.

Use Reimburse expenses on Records, select the person receiving the reimbursement, and choose outstanding expenses across suppliers. Default reimbursement payee in Settings prefills that recipient. Available credit belongs to the selected recipient.

The bank payment is allocated directly to the selected expenses. No summary, clearing or supplier-transfer records are created. You can pay part now, apply selected available credit, or settle using credit alone. Credit-only settlement creates no new bank payment.

The payment's original amount stays visible with the amount applied and current available credit. On each expense, Payments applied links to its payments. On a payment, Allocated to links to the covered expenses, including their suppliers.

Previously recorded reimbursements retain their original accounts and helper entries. Those entries remain read-only under Accounting details, with their existing correction and full-reversal safeguards. They are not converted automatically. Credits stay on their original payable account; they are not silently transferred between supplier and reimbursement balances.

Correct a reimbursement amount​

If a reimbursement payment was entered with the wrong amount, open its payment record and choose Actions → Correct reimbursement amount…. This controlled action requires a signed-in browser session and both Change records and Correct reimbursement amounts permissions. Save or discard other edits and remove any bank statement matches first.

Enter the amount actually paid and a reason of 10–2000 characters. For one covered expense, Akaun calculates the reimbursement automatically. For several expenses, enter how much was reimbursed against each one. Review the corrected payment, remaining expense balances and available credit, then choose Confirm correction.

For example, an expense costs RM5.50 but only RM5.00 was reimbursed. Correcting its payment from RM5.50 to RM5.00 makes the expense Partially paid, with RM0.50 outstanding. Use Reimburse expenses when that RM0.50 is paid later; it creates a separate payment for the new transfer. The expense cost stays RM5.50.

An increase beyond the covered expenses remains available credit for the same recipient. Previous allocations and the correction reason stay in History. The correction changes historical bank and payable balances; it does not move money at the bank.

This action supports direct reimbursements. Older reimbursements with generated payable or clearing entries, refunds and payments with dependent refund/offset allocations use their existing correction flows. If another change happens while reviewing, reload and review again.

Reverse an allocation​

If a payment is on the wrong record, reverse the allocation. On the record page or the payment page, click Correct allocation beside the allocation. Review the selected allocations and the amount that will remain unallocated, enter a reason, and confirm. You can also choose replacement allocations in the same correction. The record is then Outstanding or Partially paid again. To do this, you must have the permission to delete records.

Reverse allocation removes the allocation only. The payment record stays. Its money still moved out of the cash or bank account.

Carry an overpayment forward​

An unallocated payment is credit on the contact's account. It does not increase the original expense. Contacts show unpaid bills and invoices separately from unapplied credits, along with the net balance. Payments show Allocated or Unallocated credit.

When you record the next payment for the same contact, choose the bills or claims being paid. Under Available credit, select Apply beside the earlier payment and enter how much to use. Akaun shows the claim total, credit applied and cash remaining. It never applies credit automatically.

For example, an employee received 28.00 too much. Their next valid claim is 100.00. Apply the 28.00 credit and record a bank payment of 72.00. The expense remains 100.00. If the next claim is only 20.00, apply 20.00 credit with no cash payment; 8.00 remains for another claim.

Credits apply only to the same contact and the same payable or receivable account. Use the single-contact payment flow to apply credit. Batch payments do not automatically deduct credits.

Record an overpayment refund​

If the contact returns an overpayment, open the original overpaid payment and use Record refund. Select their credit, the amount returned, the receipt account and the actual refund date. Match the receipt to the bank statement when available.

To return a customer's excess payment, use Record refund on the original customer receipt. The form opens in the appropriate direction automatically. Refunds reduce the original credit balance. They do not create income or reverse the expense. Partial refunds leave the remaining credit available.

Correcting a reimbursement allocation​

Choose Correct allocation beside an expense on the payment. Review the affected amounts, remove or replace only the mistaken allocations, enter a reason, and confirm. The real bank payment and its bank matches stay recorded. Removed allocations become available credit for that person; the expense stays recorded under its original supplier and becomes outstanding again.

If the original expense itself was entered incorrectly, correct that separately after removing its allocations. A new reimbursement does not need a whole-group reversal or replacement helper entries merely to correct payment coverage.

Older reimbursements that contain linked transfer entries still use Reverse entire reimbursement…. The preview shows every affected expense and credit before and after; their reversal history remains visible. No replacement reimbursement is created automatically.

Paid and reconciled are different​

Paid tells that the money moved. Reconciled tells that a bank statement line matches the record. A record can be paid and Unreconciled. See Bank reconciliation.
